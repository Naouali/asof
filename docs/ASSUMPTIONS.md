# Assumptions

Where a requirement was ambiguous, the **most conservative** reading was taken —
the one that makes data look no better, and no earlier, than it really was — and
recorded here.

Correct anything here that is wrong for your purposes; these are defaults, not
convictions. Numbers are stable so a decision can be cited; gaps are entries that
belonged to parts of the project that no longer exist.

## Infrastructure

| # | Ambiguity | Choice made | Why it is the conservative one |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Python version. The spec says 3.11+. | Images pin **3.12**; `requires-python = ">=3.11,<3.13"`. | 3.12 has complete wheel coverage for the scientific stack on both architectures. The upper bound prevents a silent jump to a version some dependency has not been tested against. |
| 2 | Dependency pinning granularity. | Ranges in `pyproject.toml`, **exact pins for every package in a committed `uv.lock`**, installed with `uv sync --frozen`. | `--frozen` refuses to update the lock, so an image can never be built from a resolution that is not the committed one. CI runs `uv lock --check` so the two cannot drift. |
| 5 | Default `User-Agent`. | A string containing the literal word `unconfigured`. | `doctor` catches it before the SEC does. A plausible-looking default would get silently blocked at the first EDGAR request. |
| 6 | Whether missing API keys should block startup. | No. Every key is optional; `doctor` reports what is unavailable and why. | Spec section 10 requires the stack to run with zero keys. |
| 10 | How container health is determined. | Heartbeat files whose freshness is asserted, not PID liveness. | A deadlocked worker keeps its PID. A heartbeat check catches the case that matters. |
| 11 | `PYTHONHASHSEED`. | Pinned to `0` in the images and in CI; `doctor` warns when it is unset. | Unseeded hash randomisation varies set and dict iteration order, which is enough to change floating-point reduction order and break byte-identical results. |
| 12 | Scheduler timezone. | UTC, with market-local conversions deferred to the trading calendars. | A DST shift in a local-time cron would silently move a job across a data release. |
| 13 | Whether the data catalogue is documentation or code. | Code (`src/quantlab/data/catalogue.py`); `docs/DATA_CATALOGUE.md` is generated from it and CI fails if it is stale. | A hand-maintained caveat that has drifted is worse than no caveat: it is a false assurance. |
| 14 | pandas. | **Banned** by a ruff rule and by a test that ignores `# noqa`. | Polars' lack of an implicit index makes silent time-misalignment much harder to write. |
| 15 | Source rate limits where the provider publishes none. | A conservative self-imposed throttle per source, recorded in the catalogue (0.2–8 req/s). | Being throttled or IP-banned mid-ingest corrupts a dataset in ways that are hard to detect later. |
| 16 | Exchange settlement scraping (the only free route to a futures curve). | Catalogued, throttled to 0.5 req/s, and **disabled by default** pending a terms-of-service review. | Automated access may be prohibited. This one needs a human decision, not a default. |

## The lake, point-in-time, and the first sources

| # | Ambiguity | Choice made | Why it is the conservative one |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 17 | The spec's row contract is `as_of` + `ingested_at`. | Added a third column, **`known_at`**, and made it the point-in-time filter. `ingested_at` is kept for audit only. | Collapsing "when the world learned it" into "when we downloaded it" makes ALFRED vintages and SEC filed dates unusable — and those are the only genuine point-in-time data available for free. With one column, either historical macro backtests are impossible or they are dishonest. |
| 18 | What `as_of` means for a daily bar. | The **session close in UTC**, from the venue's trading calendar — not the session date. | A bar is not information until it has closed. A bare date makes a Tokyo close and a New York close on the "same date" look simultaneous when they are fourteen hours apart. |
| 19 | What a bare `date` means at a query boundary. | `start` widens to 00:00:00, `end` and the snapshot instant to 23:59:59.999999, all UTC. | If both widened to end-of-day, `scan(start=T, end=T)` would return nothing — a silently empty result, the exact failure this platform exists to prevent. End-of-day for the snapshot matches the spec's rebalance convention (signal on the close of T). |
| 20 | Whether an unknown exchange should default to a US calendar. | Raise `UnknownVenueError`. | Guessing a venue's hours misaligns every bar of that instrument by hours. It never raises and it always flatters. |
| 21 | Whether FRED may be used for any series. | **No.** Every series must be explicitly classified in `SERIES_POLICY` as never-revised (FRED, with a publication lag) or revised (ALFRED only). An unclassified series raises. | Pulling `GDPC1` from FRED yields a beautiful macro signal and no clue why it fails live. Forcing the classification makes the decision visible in a code review. |
| 22 | Yahoo's `adj_close` and raw prices. | Stored, and documented as restated. Total returns are to be built from `close` plus the `corporate_actions` dataset. | Yahoo restates prices for splits that happen *later* — a May 2014 Apple close is served divided by 28, covering a split six years after the bar. Returns are unaffected; price-level signals are wrong and nothing raises. |
| 23 | Stooq now serves a JavaScript proof-of-work anti-bot challenge instead of CSV. | **Do not solve it.** Detect the interstitial, fail loudly, disable the job in the shipped plan, and record the lost cross-check in LIMITATIONS.md. | It is an access control the operator deliberately deployed. Defeating it is a terms-of-service problem and gets the IP banned mid-ingest. Yahoo becomes the primary equity source and its adjustments are now unverified by an independent party — which is a real cost, stated rather than hidden. |
| 24 | How incremental ingest resumes. | From the newest `as_of` already in the lake, minus a per-job overlap window. No cursor file. | A cursor file is state that can disagree with the data it describes. The data cannot disagree with itself. The overlap is free because partition filenames are content-addressed. |
| 25 | Whether arbitrary SQL should be available to signals. | Only inside `Snapshot.sql`, on a DuckDB connection built with `enable_external_access=false` holding pre-filtered tables. | DuckDB refuses to re-enable access at runtime, so no query — however written — can reach the lake files and read around the point-in-time filter. Verified by the leakage suite. |
| 26 | Yahoo intermittently caps history at 20 years and reports `firstTradeDate` to match, so truncation is undetectable in-payload. | Request in 8-year windows (helps sometimes), verify coverage against the trading calendar, and warn when a fetch returns less history than the lake already holds. Do **not** fail the run. | Failing would make ingest unusable, since the cap is intermittent and the append-only lake means a re-run recovers the missing span. The honest cost — a first ingest can silently be short — is documented in LIMITATIONS.md with the instruction to check `data status`. |
| 27 | Whether unit tests may touch live APIs. | No. A transport-level block fails any unmarked test that opens a socket; live calls require `@pytest.mark.network`. | A test that silently hits a live API depends on a third party's uptime and on today's market data, and starts failing for reasons unrelated to the code. |

## CFTC positioning and SEC EDGAR fundamentals

| # | Ambiguity | Choice made | Why it is the conservative one |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 94 | COT `known_at`, which no CFTC payload carries. | **Derived**: the report date plus three days at 15:30 America/New_York, rolled forward over federal holidays, never backward. | The CFTC states the report is "published each Friday at 3:30 pm Eastern, using the data from the immediately preceding Tuesday". Rolling forward spends the schedule uncertainty in the direction that costs a signal performance rather than the one that manufactures it. |
| 95 | COT data before 1992-09-30. | **Refused** by default; available behind `allow_pre_weekly_era` with a 90-day punitive lag. | The CFTC states the mid-month data of that era "was not published before that time" and was compiled retrospectively. It was never knowable at its report date, so no `known_at` is honest. |
| 96 | Release time as a stored instant. | A **wall-clock** time in `America/New_York`, so 19:30 UTC in summer and 20:30 in winter. | A fixed UTC offset puts every winter release an hour early, and an hour early is enough to see a number before it exists. |
| 97 | Whether the three COT reports are three fetchers. | **One fetcher, report as an option**, matching the Binance bar interval. | The registry is keyed `<source>.<dataset>` and all three write `positioning`. Three fetchers would have broken that invariant, which exists because the lake partitions by the same key. |
| 98 | `report` in the positioning key. | **Yes.** | Legacy and disaggregated both publish a total open interest for the same contract and Tuesday, and `other_reportable` is a category in two of the three. Without it those rows share a key and deduplication keeps one taxonomy's number under the other's name. |
| 99 | Contract codes that YAML mangles. | **Rejected loudly.** | An unquoted `002602` is parsed as *octal*, giving 1410, and Socrata answers an unknown code with an empty array rather than an error. The original digits cannot be recovered from 1410, so guessing is impossible and silence cost 57% of the data. |
| 100 | EDGAR `known_at` within the filing day. | **17:30 America/New_York** on the filed date — EDGAR's acceptance cutoff. | Midnight UTC on the filed date is 8 p.m. the previous evening in New York, which would make every filing knowable before the SEC had it. |
| 101 | XBRL facts reported over several windows. | The span is classified and written into `fiscal_period`, which is part of the dataset key: `instant`, `Q`, `H`, `9M`, `FY`. | EDGAR reports 3-, 6-, 9- and 12-month revenue for the same period end in the same filing. Collapsing them mixes a quarter's revenue with a year's. |
| 102 | The year in `fiscal_period`. | **Omitted.** The label carries the span only. | `fy` on a fact is the *filing's* fiscal year, not the fact's: Apple's FY2023 revenue, restated in the FY2025 10-K, carries `fy: 2025`. The period end is already in `as_of`, so the year was both redundant and wrong. |
| 103 | A span matching no known window. | **Dropped**, not bucketed. | A seven-month figure is a transition period from a fiscal-year change; calling it a quarter or a year is wrong in both directions. |
| 104 | Several XBRL tags meaning the same metric. | **One tag per filer per metric**, by documented precedence, recorded in the log. | A filer reporting both `Revenues` and `RevenueFromContractWithCustomer...` would otherwise produce two rows under one key, and deduplication would choose between them by filing date — that is, arbitrarily. |

## CBOE volatility indices and the anomaly catalogue

| # | Ambiguity | Choice made | Why it is the conservative one |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 107 | Where VIX belongs in the lake. | **`series_observations`**, as an index level, not `ohlcv_daily`. Open, high and low are discarded. | VIX spot cannot be bought. Filing it where the engine treats it as tradeable would let a strategy collect a return nobody could have earned — the tradeable expressions are futures, options and ETPs, each with a roll cost, and all have trailed spot badly over any long horizon. With no execution possible, an intraday range has no execution meaning either. |
| 108 | VIX before September 2003. | Served, with the methodology break **logged on every fetch that spans it**. | CBOE changed to the model-free variance-swap calculation on 2003-09-22; what is served before that is a back-cast, and the index actually published then was VXO. A backtest spanning the date trades two instruments under one name. |
| 109 | VVIX's first fortnight. | **Dropped**, with the count logged. | CBOE publishes 71.73 on 6 March 2006, then a nine-day gap, then 15.71, in a series that averages 93. Serving it because the provider does would put an unusable print into any volatility-of-volatility signal. |
| 110 | A CBOE file whose layout changes. | **Raises**, naming the columns it got. | Picking whichever column looks numeric would silently load the open as the close, and a date parsed non-strictly would null rows and leave holes nobody notices. |
| 111 | Whether to keep OSAP "placebo" signals. | **Kept.** | A catalogue of what was *tried* is worth more than a catalogue of what worked. Keeping only the predictors would reproduce, inside this platform, exactly the selection that makes the published distribution misleading. |
| 112 | The two dates on a published anomaly. | `as_of` is the **end of the original sample**; `known_at` is **publication**. | They answer different questions: in-sample fit ends at the first, post-publication decay is measured from the second, and the gap between them — a median of four years — is the out-of-sample window every replication runs over. Collapsing them makes that window disappear. |
| 113 | OSAP's Google Drive distribution. | The file id is **pinned**, and an HTML response or a changed column set **raises**. | There is no versioned URL, no content hash and no API; ids change whenever the authors re-upload. Silently loading a different vintage would move every multiple-testing number derived from it without anything in the repository changing. Parsing Drive's confirmation page as CSV would yield an empty catalogue, and an empty trial count reads as "nothing has been tried". |

## Option chains and EIA

| # | Ambiguity | Choice made | Why it is the conservative one |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 114 | Where option chains come from. | **CBOE delayed quotes**, not the Yahoo endpoint the catalogue originally named. | Yahoo's options endpoint now answers unauthenticated callers with 401/429. CBOE serves the exchange's own data, with bid, ask, size, volume, open interest, implied volatility and greeks, and needs no credentials. This is a design-time source choice with the reason recorded, not a runtime substitution — spec section 13 forbids the second, not the first. |
| 115 | Whether to enable the options collector. | **Enabled.** | It is the only dataset here that cannot be backfilled: no free source sells historical chains, so the history starts when the collector starts and every day it is off is gone for good. Leaving it disabled is not a neutral default, it is a decision to have no options data for however long it stays off. |
| 116 | `as_of` for a snapshot. | CBOE's own **quote timestamp**, not the collection time. | The file is delayed. Dating a 16:15 close as though it were observed whenever the collector happened to run would misstate by hours what a snapshot is for. |
| 117 | Strikes with no open interest. | **Dropped by default**, with an opt-out. | They are the bulk of a chain and its least trustworthy part. Measured on SPX: near the money the implied-vol median is 14% with a 1.2% relative spread; below half the spot strike the implied vol reaches 449% and spreads widen to 7%, and beyond 1.5× spot the median spread is 16%. |
| 118 | CBOE's greeks and implied volatilities. | **Stored, and documented as CBOE's.** | Discarding a free field is worse than storing a documented one, but they are computed with a model, dividend assumption and rate curve that are not published alongside the numbers. A greek you did not compute is a greek whose assumptions you do not know; recompute from the mid before trading on it. |
| 119 | A malformed OCC symbol mid-snapshot. | **Skipped, counted and logged** — not raised. | One non-standard series must not abort a snapshot that cannot be taken again. The count makes a format change visible as a number rather than as silence. |
| 120 | EIA's revisions. | Ingested with `vintage=False` and a **warning on every fetch**. | EIA revises weekly inventories and serves only the current value; unlike FRED there is no ALFRED, so the first print is not archived anywhere. The look-ahead cannot be removed by this module, so the only honest option is to say so every time rather than let the row look point-in-time. |
| 121 | EIA `known_at`. | The **release instant** — Wednesday 10:30 Eastern for petroleum, Thursday for natural gas — rolled forward over federal holidays. | The period ends days before publication, so dating an observation by its period makes it knowable before the number existed. Same error as reading a COT report on its Tuesday. |
| 122 | A missing API key's error message. | Names the **source and its registration URL**, not just the unset variable. | The platform is required to run with zero keys, so this message is the normal experience of a new installation rather than an error condition. |

## Disclosed trades: insiders, institutions and Congress

| # | Ambiguity | Choice made | Why it is the conservative one |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 123 | When an SEC filing became knowable. | The submissions API's **`acceptanceDateTime`**, used as given. Absent or naive, it falls back to 17:30 Eastern on the filing date. | It is the instant EDGAR accepted the filing, to the second, and it is genuine UTC -- checked against the "Accepted" stamp on filing index pages in both summer and winter. A naive timestamp is not trusted, because guessing its zone could move a filing earlier and the fallback can only move it later. |
| 124 | Which insider lines to keep. | **All transaction lines**, with the SEC's `transaction_code`. Form 3 is not read and `...Holding` rows are skipped. | Grants, option exercises and tax withholding dominate a large issuer's Form 4s and are not a view on the stock, but deciding that is the reader's job and needs the code to do it. Holdings restate a position without a trade; filing them beside transactions would invent activity on the filing date. |
| 125 | A trade dated after the report of it. | **Dropped and counted**, not raised. | It is a typo in the filing -- "2206" for "2026" -- and cannot be placed in time. One filer's typo must not cost an issuer's whole history, and the count makes it visible. |
| 126 | Several people reporting on one Form 4. | The first is the owner by CIK; **every name is kept**; relationship flags are true if true of any. | A fund and its general partner file jointly. Keeping one name would hide who traded; one row per owner would double-count the shares. |
| 127 | The 10b5-1 flag before April 2023. | **Null**, not false. | The checkbox did not exist. Absence means "not asked", and reading it as "not planned" would label every older planned sale as discretionary. |
| 128 | What `symbol` means on a 13F. | The **manager's CIK**, written without leading zeros. Names and padded CIKs are refused. | It is what a job asks for, and incremental ingest looks the lake up by it. A 13F carries no ticker, so the security stays a CUSIP. Names are not unique and change; and unquoted in YAML a zero-padded number made of the digits 0-7 is read as octal, which silently turns one manager into another. |
| 129 | The unit of a 13F's `value`. | **Normalised to dollars by filing date**: filings made before 3 January 2023 are multiplied by 1,000. | The form changed from thousands to dollars on that date, in the same field, with nothing in the payload to say which. Left alone every position appears to grow a thousandfold overnight. |
| 130 | One 13F position split over several lines. | **Summed** per (CUSIP, put/call, share type), with the line count kept. | A manager reporting for co-managers splits a holding -- Berkshire's June 2026 filing lists Apple on twelve lines. Reading the first line as the position understates it. Puts and calls are kept apart from the shares they are written on. |
| 131 | A 13F table that disagrees with its own cover page. | **Logged**, not raised. | The totals are typed by the filer and are sometimes simply wrong. Refusing a manager's quarter over a filer's arithmetic would lose real data to a clerical error; saying nothing would hide a truncated table. |
| 132 | Which document in a 13F filing is the holdings table. | Found by **listing the filing's directory**. Two candidates raise; none means a pre-2013 text filing and is skipped. | The filer chooses the filename, so any fixed name works for some managers and silently misses others. Picking one of two candidates would load part of a portfolio as though it were all of it. |
| 133 | When a fails-to-deliver row became knowable. | The zip's **`Last-Modified`** header. A file without one is refused. | The file says nothing about when it was posted, and its settlement dates are two to six weeks earlier. The header matches the SEC's stated schedule to the day. If the SEC ever re-uploads an old file the history becomes knowable later than it was, which is the safe direction. |
| 134 | A fails-to-deliver file that is not there. | Normal for a period ending in the last **45 days**; an error for anything older. | A recent period may simply not be posted yet. A long-overdue one missing means the naming changed, and treating that as "nothing failed" would leave a silent hole. |
| 135 | Whether a fails-to-deliver download is complete. | The file's own **trailer is checked**: record count and total quantity. | A download cut short is otherwise a perfectly valid shorter file. |
| 136 | When a House report became knowable. | The **end of the filing day in Washington**. A report filed today is left for the next run. | The Clerk publishes a date with no time and no record of when a document appeared. End of day makes a 9 a.m. filing knowable fifteen hours late; midnight would make an 11 p.m. filing knowable a day early. Storing today's report now would stamp it with a `known_at` still in the future. |
| 137 | House reports that cannot be read. | They yield **no rows**, and the **filing index is stored as its own dataset** so the gap is a join away. If every report in a run is unreadable, it raises. | Roughly one report in eight is a scan with no text layer, and some of the most active traders file that way. A missing row must never read as "did not trade". A few scans are normal; all of them means the layout changed. |
| 138 | A House report whose parsed rows disagree with the dates on the page. | **Logged with the document id**; the rows that parsed are kept. | The count check is what caught transactions with no asset-type code -- one row in twenty-six of a real report. Discarding the other twenty-five to punish the one would be worse than saying so. |
| 139 | The size of a congressional trade. | Stored as the bracket: **`amount_min` and `amount_max`**, the latter null when open-ended. No midpoint. | The form reports a range whose top can be fifteen times its bottom. A midpoint is a number nobody disclosed, and it would be summed. |
| 140 | A congressional trade with no ticker. | `symbol` is **`NO_TICKER`**; the asset's name is kept. | Bonds, funds and private holdings have none. Guessing one from the name would attach a politician's municipal bond to somebody's stock. |
| 141 | House filing-type codes. | Named **only where the meaning is certain** (A, C, P, T, X). Others keep their code and get no name. | The Clerk defines them nowhere machine-readable. A guessed label is worse than an honest code. |
| 142 | The Senate (superseded by 155). | ~~Not fetched.~~ Catalogued as `senate_efd`. | The site answers automated clients with 403 and requires accepting a use agreement before any search. Getting past either is a decision for a person who has read the terms, not a default for a scheduler. The community mirrors are dead or frozen in 2020. |

## The app

| # | Ambiguity | Choice made | Why it is the conservative one |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 143 | Where the fails-to-deliver files are. | Read from the **SEC's index page**; an address is never constructed. | The SEC keeps them in four directories, out of date order -- May 2026 sits alone between April and June. A constructed address 404'd and cost a whole multi-year ingest. The page is also the authority on whether a period exists: it lists 23 files, not 24, for 2019 and 2023. A page listing under a hundred files is treated as a changed page, not as an empty history. |
| 144 | How far a 13F table may miss its cover-page total. | By **half a unit per line**. | Every line is rounded, so a table of four thousand lines honestly misses its total by a few thousand. A tolerance of one produced 38 false alarms on one manager, which is how a real mismatch gets ignored. |
| 145 | What an as-of DATE means in the app. | The **end of that day in Washington**. | EDGAR accepts until 22:00 Eastern and a House report is knowable when its filing day is over. End of day in UTC would exclude both from the day they were filed. |
| 146 | Which day an event became public on. | The **Washington date** of `known_at`, not the UTC date. | A Form 4 accepted at 21:40 Eastern is stamped 01:40 UTC the next day. The UTC date files it under a day nobody saw it, and counts its lag a day long. |
| 147 | One sale reported as many lines. | **One event** per filing, owner and transaction code, with the volume-weighted price. | A dozen fills are one decision. Twelve rows would make one seller look like a crowd. |
| 148 | What counts as routine. | Anything that is **not an open-market P or S**, anything in derivatives, and any trade flagged as under a 10b5-1 plan. Hidden by default, one click away. | It is most of what is filed and none of it is a view on the stock. Hiding it is a display default, never a deletion: the rows are all still in the lake and in the API. |
| 149 | Whether an insider filing was late. | Counted in **federal working days**, for Form 4 open-market trades only. | The rule is two business days. Counting calendar days flags every trade made on a Thursday. Form 5 and gifts are legitimately later, so they are not judged. |
| 150 | What a fund 'event' is. | The **change from the previous quarter's report**, dated at the quarter END, keeping each filing's four largest changes in the feed and ignoring moves under 10%. | A 13F lists positions, not trades, and does not say when in the quarter anything happened. The first report held opens no positions: calling all of it 'new' would be an artefact of where ingestion began. A quant manager changes thousands of lines a quarter, and a feed listing them all is a feed nobody reads; the ticker page shows every change for its own security. |
| 151 | Turning `LAST FIRST` filer names round. | Only for an officer or director, with two to four words and no corporate word. Otherwise **left as filed**. | A wrong guess renames a person or mangles a trust. |
| 152 | Honorifics and doubled words in House names. | **Dropped for display**; the member is keyed by cleaned name and district. | `Hon. Scott Scott Franklin` and `Hon. John J Mr McGuire III` are the Clerk's own values, in the index and the reports alike. Keying on the cleaned name lets a member's scanned reports and parsed trades resolve to one person. |
| 153 | Showing, in the past, what was not yet public. | Returned under its own key, `beyond`, read through a second live lens, and **drawn only behind the timeline's curtain**. Things that had not happened yet are not returned at all. | How much had already happened in secret is the reason to look at the past this way, but it must never be confusable with what was known. Everything else in the app goes through the one as-of lens. |
| 154 | Authentication. | **None yet.** Loopback by default, a warning when bound wider, `authentication: none` in the health check, the lake mounted read-only. Notes, alerts and saved views are absent. | An unaudited login is worse than an obvious loopback binding, and features that need to know who you are would be fakes without one. |
| 155 | Reaching the Senate. | **Through a mirror this project owns.** A daily GitHub Actions job reads the Senate from a US-hosted runner and commits raw report cells to the `senate-mirror` branch; the fetcher reads that by default and the Senate itself with `direct: true`. | Entry 142 recorded the 403 as a refusal of automated clients. It is a geographic block: every client outside the United States is refused, and an honest client inside is served. Other people's mirrors exist, but they are months old, unlicensed and could vanish; they were used once, to cross-check the parser. |
| 156 | The Senate's use agreement. | **Accepted by the reader**, on the owner's explicit decision (2026-09-21). | It restates 5 U.S.C. 13107(c), which already governs the House reports fetched since entry 141. Accepting it is a person's choice and was made by one; it is recorded in the catalogue as a caveat on the source. |
| 157 | `known_at` for a Senate report. | The **filing minute** printed on the report, read as Washington time; the end of the filing day when there is none (paper). | The page says "Filed 10/10/2025 @ 11:27 AM" and electronic reports are published as filed. The zone is not stated; Washington is the only sensible reading. The mirror also stores when it first saw each report, the one hard bound on publication available. |
| 158 | What the mirror stores. | The **cells of each report as the page showed them**, never interpreted rows; files change only when a report is added. | Interpretation then lives in one place and a parsing fix needs no second visit to the Senate. A file that changed every day would bury real changes in the branch's history. |
| 159 | A blank Senate ticker. | Read from the asset's name when it ends in `(TICKER)`, **except on an exchange**. | "Electronic Arts Inc. (EA)" is unambiguous. An exchange names two securities -- "(AVB) (Exchanged) ... (Received)" -- and choosing one would file the trade under the wrong company. |
| 160 | A senator's state. | **Not recorded**: `state_district` is the constant `SENATE`, shown as "Senate". | The Senate's index carries names and an office title, no state. Joining a roster would add a source to keep current for one display field. |
| 161 | `known_at` for a contract action. | The later of the action date and its first report, **plus 2 days, plus 90 days for the Department of Defense**. | The record carries no publication time. Defence actions are embargoed for 90 days by policy, confirmed on 2026-09-21: the newest defence action visible was dated 18 June, the newest civilian one 15 September. Two days stands for the nightly load. The rule errs late on purpose. |
| 162 | Which company a contract belongs to. | A **hand-curated map** of government parent identifiers (UEIs) to tickers, about forty US-listed contractors. | No official link exists, and name matching would file contracts under the wrong company. One company is spread over several parents (General Dynamics: five), so the map is by identifier. Every ticker was checked against the SEC's registrant list. |
| 163 | Joint ventures and foreign parents. | **Left out.** | A joint venture belongs to no single ticker; splitting it would invent a number. A foreign parent's US ticker is a depositary receipt, a different instrument in a different currency. |
| 164 | Small contract actions. | Dropped under **$1 million** in absolute value, configurable per job. | Most actions are administrative modifications of a few thousand dollars. De-obligations are kept: money taken back is information. |
| 165 | How USAspending is read. | Through its **download service**, not its search API. | Search returns a dozen fields and no reporting date. The download carries the full record, `initial_report_date` included, at the price of waiting half a minute for a file to be generated. |

"Everyone" means everyone who traded, and a contract is a different kind of fact. A company's own page counts every action and lists the thirty largest. |
| 167 | How a contract is drawn. | A **triangle in ink**; solid for money committed, an outline for money taken back. `direction` carries that as `buy` / `sell`. | Blue and orange mean bought and sold everywhere else in the app, and a contract is neither. The shape follows the rule the other marks follow: who by shape, which way by fill. |
| 168 | A contract and deadlines. | **None applies**; a contract is never "late". | The gap between a Pentagon action and its publication is an embargo, not a breach. Showing it as lateness would accuse an agency of following its own policy. The lag bar still shows the gap, which is the point. |
| 169 | Contracts behind the curtain. | Sent and **counted apart** from hidden trades, each capped at 120. | The embargo keeps hundreds of awards behind the curtain at any moment. Under one cap they would take every place and no hidden trade would ever be drawn. |
